How to join a national competition

Choose a competition and follow the steps in order. Teachers and students each have their own jobs. Dates change every year, so always confirm on the official website.

Bebras India Computational Thinking Challenge

Who: students aged 8 to 18 · Cost: reported as free · Mode: online, on a computer or phone · No coding knowledge needed

Teacher steps

  1. Ask your Principal for permission to join.
  2. Open bebras.cspathshala.org/admin and register the school. You become the school coordinator.
  3. After registering, upload your student information (name, class, language) using your school login link.
  4. Give each student their Login ID and password.
  5. Choose a day inside the challenge dates. Make sure the lab computers or student phones have internet.
  6. After the challenge, log in to download results and student certificates.

Student steps

  1. You do not need to study. Bring a pen and rough paper.
  2. Go to bebras.cspathshala.org and log in with your Login ID and password.
  3. Choose your language, read each puzzle slowly, and answer.

Reported dates for 2026: challenge 16 November to 5 December, school registration until 28 November. Confirm on the official Bebras India page.

INSPIRE Awards – MANAK

Who: classes 6 to 10, age 10 to 15 · Run by the Department of Science and Technology

  1. Principal or an authorised teacher registers the school one time on inspireawards-dst.gov.in.
  2. Student picks a real problem around them and thinks of an original solution. It must be the student's own idea.
  3. Student writes a short description with a drawing, photo, or short video of the idea.
  4. When nominations open, the school nominates up to 5 best ideas online on the E-MIAS portal. Students cannot apply alone.
  5. Selected ideas receive an award and can go to district, state, and national exhibitions.

The 2026-27 nomination window has closed (it ended 15 September 2026). Register the school now so you are ready when the next window opens.

National Children's Science Congress (NCSC)

Who: age 10 to 17 · Run by NCSTC, Department of Science and Technology · Usually held in December

  1. Read this year's theme and the Activity Guidebook 2026-27. The 2026 theme is reported as "Science and Innovation for Sustainable Development".
  2. Form a team and choose one local problem in your area that fits the theme.
  3. Investigate like a scientist: observe, collect data, record results, and draw a conclusion.
  4. Write a short project report and prepare a model or poster.
  5. Ask your Principal to find your district and state coordinator through ncsc.co.in.
  6. Present at district level. The best projects go to state level, and then national level.

Coding competitions that lead to the world

Indian Computing Olympiad (ICO), run by IARCS

Who: school students up to Class 12, any board (2026-27 cycle) · Individual contest · Path to IOI

  1. Learn to solve problems with code. Start with loops, conditions, lists and sorting.
  2. Practise with old ZCO and INOI question papers. They are available online on the IARCS website.
  3. Register for the first round, ZCO or ZIO, when registration opens. Check the IARCS site for dates and your school's rules.
  4. Top students go to the Indian National Olympiad in Informatics (INOI).
  5. About 30 top INOI students attend the IOI training camp. Four students are chosen to represent India at the International Olympiad in Informatics (IOI).

International Olympiad in AI (IOAI)

  1. Students register one by one. The school cannot register everyone at once, but teachers can share the information and guide students.
  2. Read the rules and the FAQ on the ACM India IOAI page. Only Indian citizens can go on to represent India.

Tip: start with Bebras. It needs no coding and builds the thinking skills these olympiads test.

ATL Marathon

Who: school students, grades 6 to 12 · Run by Atal Innovation Mission, NITI Aayog

  1. Open aim.gov.in/atl.php and read who can apply. Many parts are for schools with an Atal Tinkering Lab.
  2. Choose a problem and build a working prototype of your solution.
  3. Record your work with photos and a short video.
  4. Submit through the AIM website when the challenge opens.
